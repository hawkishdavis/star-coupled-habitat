The question

Rings spinning in different planes each carry an angular momentum vector pointing a different way. Couple them, or nudge one, and it answers by twisting perpendicular to the push. Can the field geometry be arranged so a set of non-coplanar levitated rings stays put, or does every arrangement pay for stability with continuous power?

Kellie Peterson, Star-Coupled Rotational Habitat, v0.2 draft, July 2026

Two AI reviewers flagged it. A real geometer has not. That is the ask.

Three ways to build it

Empirical

Single shell, one axis

The Bernal sphere, 1929. 500 m at 1.9 rpm gives one g at the equator and nothing at the poles, for free. Must be shaped wider than tall or the intermediate-axis theorem flips the floor into a wall.

Experimental

Star topology, one plane

Every ring couples to one outer driver, not to its neighbor. The driver is flywheel, gravity source, and attitude control at once, which NASA already does in one device class. Not in the literature as a habitat.

Aspirational

Cage in cage, many planes

Great-circle bands, no shared axis, coupled by field not by adjacency. Downgraded on review: each ring precesses, so the suspension must actively hold it. Power cost unknown. This is the model above.

What the numbers are doing

Effective gravity at the rim is g = ω²r, with ω in radians per second. Comfort literature caps rotation near 2 rpm; above that the Coriolis twist becomes noticeable when you move toward or away from the axis. At 2 rpm, one full g wants a radius of about 224 m. Smaller rings still work, they just deliver partial gravity or ask for adaptation. The sliders hold to that scale.

Empirical

Rotational artificial gravity, magnetic bearings, flywheel energy storage, momentum-exchange attitude control. Flight-proven or studied by NASA and ESA for decades.

Experimental

Star-topology coupling, polarity-reversal momentum transfer, waste as ballast. Sound in isolation, unmodeled at habitat scale.

Aspirational

Non-coplanar cage, rings as radiation shield. Kept in the record as ideas. A Faraday cage stops fields, not cosmic rays.
